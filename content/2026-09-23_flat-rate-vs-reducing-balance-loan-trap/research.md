# Research — Flat rate vs reducing balance loan trap

Topic: why a personal loan advertised at a "12% flat rate" can actually
cost close to double that in real interest.

## Core mechanism

- **Reducing balance (diminishing balance) interest**: interest is charged
  only on the principal that is still outstanding. As you repay each EMI,
  the base on which interest is calculated shrinks every month. This is the
  method almost all scheduled commercial banks in India use for personal
  loans (SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank, etc.).
- **Flat rate interest**: interest is calculated once, on the full original
  loan amount, and charged for the entire tenure — even though the
  outstanding balance keeps shrinking every month as EMIs are paid. This
  method is more common among smaller NBFCs, microfinance lenders, and some
  fintech/app-based lenders.
- Because the flat-rate method keeps charging interest on money you've
  already partly repaid, the *effective* (reducing-balance-equivalent)
  interest rate on a flat-rate loan is materially higher than the quoted
  number — commonly cited as roughly **1.7x to 2x** the flat rate for
  typical personal-loan tenures.

## Confirmed figures

- A widely cited illustrative comparison: a **12% flat-rate** loan works
  out to an **effective rate of roughly 21–22%** on a reducing-balance
  basis (example used: ₹3 lakh loan over 3 years).
  Source: mybankingtips.com, "Flat Rate vs Reducing Balance Rate in
  Personal Loans: Which One Costs More?" — https://www.mybankingtips.com/personal-loan/flat-rate-vs-reducing-balane-rate-in-personal-loan
- Multiple lender-published explainers converge on the same shape of claim:
  "a low flat rate can cost 70–100% more in interest than the same quoted

## Why this trap works on borrowers

- Flat rate is quoted as a single, smaller-looking number ("12% p.a."),
  which reads as cheaper than a bank's reducing-balance offer of, say,
  "16% p.a.", even though the flat-rate loan is the more expensive one once
  converted to an apples-to-apples basis.
- Loan marketing rarely states "flat" or "reducing" prominently; it is often
  in the fine print of the sanction letter or loan agreement, not the
  advertised headline rate.
- Borrowers comparing offers across a bank and an NBFC/fintech app are
  frequently comparing a reducing rate to a flat rate without realising the
  two numbers are not computed the same way.

## Notes on claim handling for the carousel

- Use the ~12% flat -> ~21-22% reducing-equivalent figures as one
  illustrative worked example (consistent across multiple sources), not a
  claim about any single specific lender's current pricing.
- Do not name a specific bank or NBFC as "the one that does this" — frame it
  as a lending-method distinction, since both banks and NBFCs can technically
  offer either method; the pattern cited (banks lean reducing, smaller
  NBFCs/fintechs lean flat) is a general market tendency, not a rule.
- No specific date-bound regulatory claim is used (e.g. no claim that flat
  rate disclosure is illegal or banned); the carousel's claim is limited to
  the mathematical difference between the two methods and the fact that the
  effective cost is meaningfully higher under flat rate.
