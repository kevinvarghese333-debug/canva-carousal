# Research notes: Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) invisible debt

## Why this topic
Recurring @whenkevintalks carousels so far have covered merchant no-cost EMI,
credit card due-date psychology, credit bureau reporting, CTC vs take-home,
F&O trading, minimum balance penalties, ULIPs, flat-rate vs reducing-balance
loans, expense ratios and digital gold spreads. BNPL checkout-credit apps
(distinct from merchant no-cost EMI) are a natural next entry in the
"lending and credit traps" pillar: near-universal checkout experience,
doesn't require citing a specific regulator or company, and gives a strong
built-in visual contrast (one small purchase vs. several stacked
obligations).

## Core facts used (kept generic/illustrative, not attributed to a specific app)
- BNPL ("Pay in 3", "Pay Later" checkout options) is a short-term consumer
  credit product, functionally a small loan, even though it is presented as
  a payment method rather than a loan application.
- A single BNPL purchase is usually small enough to not feel like debt, but
  using multiple BNPL apps at once means multiple simultaneous repayment
  obligations that are easy to lose track of because they aren't consolidated
  anywhere the way a single loan statement would be.
- Missing a BNPL due date typically triggers a late fee, and many BNPL
  providers now report repayment behaviour to credit bureaus, so a missed
  payment can affect a credit score much like a missed loan EMI.
- Paid on time and used deliberately, BNPL can be genuinely interest-free
  credit; the risk described here is about stacking multiple obligations
  and losing visibility, not a claim that BNPL is inherently predatory.

## Why the framing avoids over-claiming
- No specific BNPL provider, app, fee schedule, or regulatory citation is
  used. The ₹500 instalment and ₹15,000-across-four-apps example are
  explicitly round, illustrative numbers.
- The carousel does not state a universal late fee amount or a specific
  credit bureau reporting rule as fact; it treats late fees and bureau
  reporting as a general, real, but provider-specific outcome.
- Slide 7 explicitly presents the upside (interest-free when paid on time)
  so the carousel argues against invisible stacking, not against BNPL as a
  product category.

## Sources consulted
General knowledge of how BNPL/checkout-credit products are structured and
commonly discussed (short-term instalment credit, distinct from traditional
personal loans, with late-fee and credit-bureau-reporting practices varying
by provider). No external web research was performed for this run; no
company-specific or time-sensitive regulatory claims are made, so none was
required to keep the content accurate.
