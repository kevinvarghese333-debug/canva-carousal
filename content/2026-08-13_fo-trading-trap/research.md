# Research notes: F&O (futures & options) individual trader losses

## Why this topic
Recurring @whenkevintalks carousels so far have covered lending and pay
mechanics (no-cost EMI, on-time credit card trap, RBI weekly credit
reporting, CTC vs take-home pay). F&O trading is a natural next entry in
the "financial traps" pillar: it is timely (SEBI has run and updated this
study across FY22-FY24 and FY24-25), has an official regulator source
(avoids the "guru nonsense" framing the brand explicitly rejects), and
gives a strong built-in visual contrast (93% vs 7%, and retail losses vs
institutional profits on the same trades).

## Core facts used (all traceable to SEBI's study, first published and
then updated through 2024-2025)
- SEBI's study "Analysis of Profit and Loss of Individual Traders dealing
  in Equity F&O Segment" was updated in September 2024, covering FY22 to
  FY24 (three financial years), based on more than 1 crore individual
  traders.
- 93% of individual traders in the equity F&O segment incurred losses
  over that three-year period.
- Aggregate losses of individual traders across the three years exceeded
  ₹1.8 lakh crore.
- Loss-making traders lost an average of about ₹2,00,000 each over the
  period.
- Only about 1% of individual traders made a profit exceeding ₹1,00,000
  after accounting for transaction costs.
- In FY24, proprietary trading firms and foreign portfolio investors
  (FPIs) recorded gross trading profits of about ₹33,000 crore and
  ₹28,000 crore respectively (before transaction costs) in the same
  derivatives market where most individual traders lost money.
- A later SEBI update (covering FY24-25, released mid-2025) found losses
  continuing to widen: about 91% of individual traders lost money, with
  net losses rising to roughly ₹1.05 lakh crore in FY24-25 (up from about
  ₹74,800 crore in FY23-24), and an average per-trader loss of about
  ₹1.1 lakh. This later figure is not used as a headline stat in the
  carousel to avoid mixing two different study periods' numbers in a
  9-slide format; it is noted here as directional confirmation that the
  core finding (most individual F&O traders lose) is not a one-off result.

## Why the framing avoids over-claiming
- The 93% figure, the ₹1.8 lakh crore aggregate loss, the ₹2,00,000
  average loss, the 1% profit threshold, and the FY24 proprietary/FPI
  profit figures are all taken from the SEBI study period they belong to
  (FY22-FY24, or FY24 specifically for the proprietary/FPI figures) and
  are not blended with the separate, later FY24-25 update.
- The carousel does not claim individual traders are unsophisticated or
  imply the losses are due to a single cause; slide 7 frames the result
  as partly structural (institutional participants profiting on the same
  trades), consistent with SEBI's own commentary about transaction costs
  and the structural position of individual option buyers.
- No specific broker, app, or individual is named.
