# Research notes: ULIPs sold as insurance and investment together

## Why this topic
Carousels so far have covered lending and credit traps (EMI, credit card,
RBI credit reporting), a banking fee trap (minimum balance), a trading
trap (F&O), and a salary-structuring trap (CTC vs take-home). Insurance is
an untouched pillar in the "money decisions" bucket, and ULIPs (Unit
Linked Insurance Plans) are the most commonly mis-sold product in that
category: they are pitched as "insurance and investment in one," which
sounds efficient but structurally works against the buyer in the early
years. It gives a strong built-in contrast for the visual system (premium
paid vs. amount actually invested), similar in shape to the CTC carousel's
"headline number vs. real number" tension.

## Core facts used (kept generic/illustrative, not attributed to a specific insurer or product)
- A ULIP bundles two things inside one premium: a life insurance
  component and a market-linked investment component (units in a fund).
- Before any premium reaches the investment fund, several charges are
  deducted: premium allocation charge, policy administration charge, fund
  management charge, and mortality charge (the cost of the life cover
  itself). These charges are typically front-loaded, i.e. highest as a
  share of premium in the early policy years.
- ULIPs carry a mandatory minimum lock-in period (commonly 5 years).
  Surrendering before the lock-in period ends moves the fund value into a
  discontinuance fund and can attract a surrender/discontinuance charge,
  on top of having missed years of compounding.
- Because a ULIP prices in a life-cover cost and multiple product charges
  inside one number, it is difficult to compare cleanly against buying a
  separate term insurance policy and a separate market investment (e.g. an
  index fund or mutual fund), which is why direct like-for-like comparison
  is the practical takeaway rather than a specific return-percentage claim.

## Why the framing avoids over-claiming
- No specific insurer, plan name, IRDAI circular, or charge percentage is
  cited as fact. The "₹1,00,000 premium, only part of it invested" example
  is explicitly a round, illustrative number to show the mechanism, not a
  claim about any specific plan's charge structure.
- The carousel does not claim ULIPs are always a bad product or state a
  universal "expected loss" figure; it treats the early-year charge drag
  and lock-in as a real, structural feature of the product category that
  is worth understanding before signing, consistent with the brand's
  "no hidden number changed" rule.
- The 5-year lock-in is stated as a common feature of the product category
  rather than tied to a specific regulation date, avoiding a time-sensitive
  regulatory claim that would need fresh verification.

General knowledge of how ULIPs are structured (allocation charge, policy
