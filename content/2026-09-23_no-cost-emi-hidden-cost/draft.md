# No Cost EMI: The Interest You Still Pay

## Slide 1 — Cover
Background: navy, typography-first, left-aligned.

"No Cost EMI."
Nothing about this is free.

Tag: THE CHECKOUT LIE

## Slide 2 — Problem
Background: off-white.

You are one tap from checkout.
A toggle appears: "No Cost EMI available."

You click it without thinking twice.

Tag: CHECKOUT

## Slide 3 — Setup
Background: off-white, large navy headline (huge-typography slide).

Interest doesn't disappear.
It changes its name.

Small tag: 0% ≠ FREE

## Slide 4 — Mechanism
Background: navy. Bar/price comparison, huge figures as visual objects.

Label: THE SWAP

The seller pays your bank the interest for you.
The bank pays for it by removing the discount you'd have gotten in cash.

Bar 1: CASH PRICE (WITH DISCOUNT) — shorter bar
Bar 2: "NO COST" EMI PRICE (NO DISCOUNT) — full-width bar

## Slide 5 — Example
Background: off-white. Itemised receipt / mechanism proof slide.

Tag: THE RECEIPT

An illustrative ₹30,000 purchase, "no cost" EMI, broken down.

Processing fee, GST on the bank's interest, and cashback you don't earn
on EMI transactions. None of these are waived.

## Slide 6 — Reveal
Background: navy. Deliberately minimal, centred, high tension.

The 0% is real.
The free part isn't. (red emphasis)

Tag: READ THE FINE PRINT

## Slide 7 — Insight
Background: off-white.

This isn't a scam. It's a trade.

Tag: THE UPSIDE (green)

Spreading a cost over months has real value.
Just don't mistake it for a discount. (green emphasis)

## Slide 8 — Takeaway
Background: navy, gold warning/detail block.

Before you tap "No Cost EMI," check three things:

Tag: THE CHECK ✓

01  Ask for the cash price, not just the EMI price.
02  Check the processing fee and GST on the interest.
03  Confirm your card's cashback still applies.
