# Research — "No Cost EMI" hidden cost

Topic: how "No Cost EMI" checkout offers actually work, and where the
supposedly-waived interest really goes.

## Core mechanism (subvention model)

- "No cost EMI" does not mean the bank or NBFC lends money for free. It means
  the interest is not charged to you directly. Instead the seller (retailer,
  brand, or marketplace) pays the bank/NBFC a lump sum called an "interest
  subvention" that covers the interest the bank would otherwise have earned.
  The bank then shows your EMI schedule as 0% interest.
- Sellers recover that subvention cost by not extending you the cash/upfront
  discount they would otherwise give, or by pricing the product slightly
  higher for EMI checkout than for full upfront payment. The "discount" and
  the "interest" are two labels for the same money — whichever one you are
  shown depends on how you pay.
- The Reserve Bank of India flagged as early as 2013 that truly interest-free
  retail finance schemes are not economically possible and pushed for the
  real cost to be disclosed to the borrower rather than silently loaded into
  the product's price.
  Source: Business Standard, "RBI cracks whip on retail finance schemes with
  hidden cost" — https://www.business-standard.com/article/finance/rbi-cracks-whip-on-retail-finance-schemes-with-hidden-cost-113092500706_1.html

## Extra costs layered on top of the subvention swap

1. **GST on the interest component.** Even though the interest is marketed as
   waived, the bank books a notional interest amount internally for the EMI
   conversion, and 18% GST is charged on that notional interest — this GST is
   passed to the customer and is not waived.
2. **Processing fee.** Banks/NBFCs typically charge a flat EMI-conversion or
   processing fee (commonly in the roughly ₹99–₹999 range depending on the
   issuer and ticket size), charged regardless of whether the interest itself
   is waived, and usually non-refundable even on early foreclosure.
3. **Foregone rewards.** Most card issuers exclude EMI-converted transactions
   from reward points, cashback, or milestone benefits that would apply to a
   normal full-payment purchase on the same card.
4. **Rigid tenure.** The "no cost" pricing is calculated for a specific
   tenure; foreclosing early or changing the plan can trigger the deferred
   interest/discount being clawed back as a foreclosure charge in some
   issuer terms.

## Net effect (illustrative, not a specific-merchant claim)

Multiple consumer-finance explainers converge on the same shape of example:
a product with a meaningful cash/full-payment discount (illustratively
~5% of price) ends up priced at or near MRP under "no cost EMI," and the
processing fee + GST on interest + lost cashback together add up to a few
hundred to a couple of thousand rupees of real cost on a mid-size purchase
(commonly cited illustrative range: ~₹500–₹1,200 extra on a ~₹30,000
purchase). The number varies by issuer, product, and ticket size — this
carousel uses one round, clearly illustrative figure rather than citing any
single retailer or bank's real pricing.

## Notes on claim handling for the carousel

- Do not name a specific bank, card issuer, or retailer as an example.
- Do not state processing-fee or GST rupee amounts as universal facts; frame
  them as "commonly" charged / illustrative, since exact fees vary by issuer.
- The RBI reference is used narrowly: RBI flagged the "0% interest" framing
  as economically misleading and pushed for disclosure — this carousel does
  not claim RBI has banned no-cost EMI outright, since practice today is
  that issuers disclose the subvention/discount trade-off rather than
  removing the offer.
