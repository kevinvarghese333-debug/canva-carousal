# Research notes: CTC vs take-home pay

## Why this topic
Recurring @whenkevintalks carousels so far (EMI hidden cost, on-time credit
card trap, RBI weekly credit reporting) have covered lending and credit
traps. CTC vs take-home pay is a natural next entry in the "money decisions"
pillar: it's a near-universal first encounter with financial fine print
(every salaried person reads an offer letter), doesn't require citing a
specific regulator/date (unlike the RBI carousel), and gives a strong
built-in contrast for the visual system (one big number vs a smaller real
number).

## Core facts used (kept generic/illustrative, not attributed to a specific employer)
- CTC (Cost to Company) is an aggregate figure that typically includes:
  basic pay, HRA and other allowances, employer's PF contribution,
  gratuity accrual, and variable/bonus components.
- Take-home pay is only the components that are actually paid out monthly
  (broadly: basic + allowances, minus employee-side deductions).
- Employer PF contribution and gratuity are real money credited in the
  employee's name, but are not part of monthly take-home — PF is
  withdrawable under specific conditions, gratuity vests only after
  completing a minimum service period (commonly 5 years under the
  Payment of Gratuity Act framework) or on specified exits.
- Bonus/variable pay is often performance- or company-linked and not
  guaranteed in full.

## Why the framing avoids over-claiming
- No specific company, offer, or regulatory citation is used — the ₹12,00,000
  example is explicitly a round, illustrative number, consistent with the
  brand's "no hidden number changed" rule while not requiring net-new
  factual verification of a live regulation (unlike the RBI carousel).
- The carousel does not state an exact universal "gap percentage" as fact;
  it treats the CTC-to-take-home gap as a real but example-specific and
  variable outcome, avoiding a claim that could be wrong for any given reader.

## Sources consulted
General knowledge of standard Indian salary structuring (CTC / basic / HRA /
PF / gratuity conventions) and the Payment of Gratuity Act's 5-year vesting
convention. No external web research was performed for this run; no
company-specific or time-sensitive regulatory claims are made, so none was
required to keep the content accurate.
