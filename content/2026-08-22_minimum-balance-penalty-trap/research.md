# Research notes: minimum balance penalty on savings accounts

## Why this topic
Prior @whenkevintalks carousels have covered credit-side traps (no-cost EMI,
on-time credit card interest, RBI weekly credit reporting), a salary-side
trap (CTC vs take-home pay), and a trading-side trap (F&O trading). The
minimum balance penalty is a savings-account trap that almost every account
holder has been charged at least once, giving it the same "near-universal
first encounter" quality as the CTC carousel, while opening a new corner of
the "money decisions" pillar: everyday banking fees rather than credit or
investing products.

## Core facts used (kept generic/illustrative, not attributed to a specific bank)
- Most Indian savings accounts (particularly regular, non-zero-balance
  accounts) require customers to maintain a Monthly Average Balance (MAB),
  commonly called Average Monthly Balance (AMB), rather than a fixed
  balance on any single day.
- The requirement varies by bank and branch location tier (metro/urban,
  semi-urban, rural) and by account variant.
- If the average balance across the month falls below the required level,
  banks levy a non-maintenance charge, typically structured as a slab or
  percentage of the shortfall, plus applicable GST.
- These charges are usually deducted automatically at the end of a
  quarter (or month, depending on the bank) without a separate approval
  step from the customer, and often appear only as a line item in the
  account statement.
- Zero-balance accounts (such as Basic Savings Bank Deposit Accounts),
  many salary accounts, and some digital-first bank accounts waive the
  minimum balance requirement entirely, which is the actionable
  "workaround" used on slide 7.

## Why the framing avoids over-claiming
- No specific bank, branch tier, or fee schedule is cited. The ₹10,000
  required average / ₹6,400 actual average / ₹590 charge figures are
  explicitly a round, illustrative example, consistent with the brand's
  "no hidden number changed" rule, mirroring how the CTC carousel used an
  illustrative ₹12,00,000 CTC figure rather than a sourced statistic.
- The carousel does not claim a universal minimum balance amount or a
  universal penalty amount as fact; it presents the AMB mechanism (average,
  not single-day balance) as the real and generalizable trap, and treats
  the exact numbers as example-specific.
- The slide 7 "workaround" claim (zero-balance/salary accounts waive the
  fee) is a widely known, non-time-sensitive feature of how Indian banks
  structure account types, not a claim tied to a specific regulation or
  date, so it does not require live citation to stay accurate.
