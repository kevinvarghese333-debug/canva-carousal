# Research notes: digital gold's buy-sell spread

## Why this topic
The recurring @whenkevintalks carousel has so far covered lending traps (EMI,
credit card, RBI reporting, flat-rate loans), pay traps (CTC vs take-home),
and fund-fee traps (expense ratio, ULIP, minimum balance). Digital gold is a
natural next entry in the "money decisions" pillar: apps like Google Pay,
PhonePe and Paytm have made buying "digital gold" a one-tap habit, it is
marketed as a safe, simple way to save, and almost nobody checks the sell
price before they buy. It also matches the brand's own listed gold motif
(price tag / coin edge / buy-button) so it fits the visual system well.

## Core facts used (kept generic/illustrative, not attributed to a specific app or a live price)
- Digital gold sold through payment apps is typically issued by a bullion
  partner (e.g. MMTC-PAMP, SafeGold, Augmont) and is not itself a product
  regulated by SEBI or RBI; this lack of direct regulation has been publicly
  flagged by financial commentators and by SEBI's own investor-caution
  messaging around unregulated gold-buying schemes.
- Platforms quote a "buy" price and a separate, lower "sell-back" price for
  the same gram of gold at the same moment — a bid-ask spread, on top of
  which 3% GST applies to the purchase (GST on gold under Indian tax rules)
  and is not refunded if the gold is sold back.
- Because of the spread plus GST, selling digital gold back immediately
  after buying it typically returns less than the amount originally paid,
  even if the underlying gold price has not moved.
- Physical redemption (converting digital gold into a coin/bar) usually adds
  further making/delivery charges.

## Why the framing avoids over-claiming
- No exact spread percentage, GST rate change, or platform name is asserted
  as a live, current figure; the ₹10,000 example and the illustrative
  buy/sell numbers are round, hypothetical figures used to demonstrate the
  mechanism, not a quote from any specific app on any specific date.
- The carousel does not claim digital gold is a scam or that all platforms
  behave identically — it isolates one specific, structural cost (the
  spread + non-refundable GST) that is broadly true of the category rather
  than a claim that could be wrong for any one provider.

## Sources consulted
General knowledge of how digital gold products are sold in India (buy/sell
spread, GST treatment on gold purchases, lack of a dedicated SEBI/RBI
regulatory framework for digital gold). No external web research was
performed for this run; no platform-specific pricing, regulatory citation,
or time-sensitive figure is used, so none was required to keep the content
